# C-3 Verification Interrogation

## Definition

Any assertion entering a decision chain must first pass two interrogations: **① where is the evidence** — is it a firsthand-verified fact, or a relay, an inference, a performance? **② who can re-verify** — is its verification independently repeatable, or self-attested only?

## Judgment

- The first question adjudicates the source; the second, the path. Both clear — the assertion may ground a decision; either vague — the assertion is downgraded to "unverified information" and must not drive action.
- The two interrogations grade naturally — the **verification levels**:

| Level | Name | Test |
|---|---|---|
| L0 | Secondhand citation | Unverified relay (AI summaries, hearsay) |
| L1 | Existence check | The object exists; metadata firsthand |
| L2 | Firsthand content | The material itself read, cited to file/line |
| L3 | Independent re-verification | A second executor, different method, same conclusion |

- When citing others' conclusions, the level of a chain is that of its lowest link — **an L2 conclusion citing L0 material is treated overall as L0**.
- The criterion in daily form: facing anything that "looks valid," do not first ask what it says — first ask on what grounds it is believed.

## Origin

2026-09 batch adjudication in collaborative practice: while establishing a ground-truth baseline for a set of conclusive assertions, it emerged that most disagreement was not about opinions but about evidence levels — hence "ask for evidence, ask for re-verification" distilled into the two-interrogation criterion; later incorporated into the criteria library alongside the unified criterion.

## Relation to the Seed

The daily tool of the differential branch: the "checkable, hard-to-forge" character of differential change, in concrete use, is precisely these two interrogations — where is the evidence (checkable), who can re-verify (hard to forge).
