# The Shifting Sense of the Real: Sanderson × Zeng Ming — Experience and Industry in the AI Era

> **The Evolution Archive · Part One · Entry 2**. The second discussion of 2026-09-13, following the first half of the entropy-barrier session. Original text, sanitized; editor's note at the end.

---

**The question**: two source materials — one humanistic, one business — seemingly unrelated. Where do they converge, and what does the convergence mean for "people's experience" and "the information industry" in the AI era?

## 1. The Convergence Thesis: Value Pricing Migrates from Product Appearance to Process Evidence

The two articles are two faces of one coin:

- **Sanderson (experience side)**: as AI drives the cost of "obtaining the product" toward zero, the anchor of human experience's value is forced from product to process — "the book is the receipt; you are the artwork."
- **Zeng Ming (industry side)**: homogeneous model supply earns no excess profit (the refinery); the agent company's moat lies in context management, memory, interaction — that is, **process assets**.

Merged into one sentence: **when products (content / code / analysis) can be synthesized without limit, the market's pricing anchor migrates from "product appearance" to "process evidence" — the part that cannot be synthesized and can only be bought with time.** This is the mechanism core of "the shifting sense of the real": **the sense of the real is not a moral judgment; it is scarcity pricing.**

## 2. Sanderson: Process as Ontology (four points, experience side)

1. **The product/process distinction is the axis**. AI products treat art as mere product (replicable output), while art's ontology is process (the process that shapes the creator). Proof by elimination: resource use, copyright, replacing humans, lower quality — none is the crux. The crux is AI skipping the journey of becoming a creator.
2. **The Data vs LLM thought experiment**: both non-human creators, yet Data is acknowledged because it "creates to grow and is changed by creating"; an LLM writes a shopping list no differently than a loved one's death — "It will not learn. It will not grow. It will not care. It cannot be changed by creation." The test is not intelligence level but **the capacity to be changed by the process**.
3. **The real danger is voluntarily surrendering the process**: what AI takes is not the work but the growth opportunity — on the condition that one outsources the five practice pieces oneself. The impoverishment of experience is not deprivation; it is voluntary.
4. **Society retains the right to define**: art is defined and given meaning collectively; humans can choose "the definition of victory."

## 3. Zeng Ming: The Organization as Generator (five points, industry side)

1. **A three-stage industry history**: infrastructure → massive applications → native applications; tokens becoming the unit of measure (the "kilowatt-hour") marks stage one closing; the agent boom analogizes to building websites in 1992. **Surviving across stages is the exception.**
2. **Model companies are fated to be infrastructure**: AOL-style access providers; a trillion-dollar valuation does not change the refinery essence; "oil companies cannot make cars."
3. **The next niche is the agent's "browser"**: unified standard + user entry point; currently "no prototype at all in sight"; may appear within three years.
4. **Posts → tasks; company → generative organization**: strategy shifts from planning to **emerging** from the human-machine collaboration network — "the organization is the generative system of strategy"; the watershed is whether the CEO personally rewrites how the organization runs with AI.
5. **Intelligence compounding + the black-hole effect**: the moat shifts from data flywheel to intelligence flywheel — context management, memory, interaction; not the model itself.

## 4. The Three-Layer Framework (the discussion's skeleton)

| Layer | Question | Core judgment |
|---|---|---|
| **Experience** | As products cheapen and process appreciates, where does human time and spending reallocate? | Consumptive experience (AI can do it) loses value; formative experience (the process shapes the person) becomes scarce — the Sanderson domain |
| **Trust** | After synthetic content floods, "proving a real process behind it" becomes a universal need | **Process proof is the base component of the sense-of-the-real economy**: trajectory and verification upgrade from internal quality tools to external capability |
| **Industry** | Who captures the niche of "verifying the real / credible process"? | Zeng Ming's "browser" is most likely not a traffic portal but a **trust portal**: whoever proves agent processes credible at scale takes the entry point |

## 5. Implications for the Product Line (first draft)

1. **A process-first architecture is a structural tailwind**: dispatch, trajectory, verification, attribution as process architecture from day one — self-proof is not a marketing posture but product positioning riding "value migrating to process."
2. **Externalizing awareness capabilities**: trajectory protocols, reliability metrics, failure taxonomy, attribution pipelines — internally quality infrastructure, externally the technical base of process proof. Direction: capability first, narrative as its rendering layer.
3. **The skin layer's new duty**: the interface is not dashboard cosmetics — it is the UI of "process visibility." **Making the process visible = making the sense of the real perceivable.**
4. **The long-term interface of creative identity**: "human creative identity is a scarce asset; platforms should protect and amplify the visibility of process" — the 3D/gaming counterpart is the concretization and verifiability of the creative process (replay, timelines, auditable asset history).

## 6. Toward the Four-Line Convergence (that evening's next step)

The same evening: beyond Sanderson / Zeng Ming, **LeCun and Sutton** explore routes beyond the LLM architecture, emphasizing the **world model** — four lines might be strung together in one larger picture.

**The convergence hypothesis** (a preliminary judgment at the time, not a conclusion): the four lines point at one gap — **the current LLM is "a product engine without a process."** For humans (Sanderson): creators who were never shaped. For organizations (Zeng Ming): hierarchies without generative capacity. For models (LeCun/Sutton): language statistics without world model or experience. And "process-nature" — the capacity to be changed by the process — is exactly what the whole system (trajectory / awareness / memory / evolution) is building for the agent. The agent's "world model" need not be pixels or physical space; it may be **the state space of a working process** — isomorphic to JEPA's "predicting in an abstract representation space."

(The hypothesis was formalized the next day as the four-scale unified criterion, "process-nature = path-dependent negentropy accumulation" — see Part Two, entry 4.)
